DESIGN PLAN · VERSION 1 · 4 NOVEMBER 2026

Idle Restaurant Tycoon

A warm, readable 3D restaurant empire built around Traffic Flow’s three-stage throughput economy. Customers order, the kitchen prepares and delivers food, diners finish, and a completed service wave earns a bonus.

Design decision

Preserve the original progression structure and numerical baseline first. Give each bottleneck a restaurant meaning, then validate timing and completion pace with a restaurant simulation. Visual polish must make congestion, available upgrades and cash rewards obvious. This plan does not claim that a theme change alone preserves total playtime.

Source means a recovered value from the supplied Traffic Flow 0.8.1 GDD and balance workbook. Proposed means a restaurant design choice. Demo means a staged presentation event rather than a production economy rule.

Visual direction

Use Eatventure as a reference for the elevated cutaway camera, simple rounded people, legible stations and visible staff routes. Use Cookingdom as a reference for appetising food variety and warm, cozy colour relationships. Cookingdom’s illustrated food presentation is translated into original 3D dishes; its manual cooking mini-games are not part of the baseline idle loop.

Soft directional shadows, restrained highlights, warm whites and cool muted shadows. Food uses a stronger colour range than the room: toasted buns, tomato reds, leaf greens, creamy pasta, golden fries and berry desserts. Food silhouettes should remain identifiable on a phone. Avoid photorealism, heavy bloom and the previous neon art direction.

Core loop and exact reuse boundary

ORDER
Host and cashier→COOK AND DELIVER
Chef and server→DINE AND CLEAR
Dining service

Guests enter a bounded service wave. The three stages process concurrently. Visual queues reveal the slowest stage. Revenue is credited at the same logical stage events as the source. Finishing every cover in the wave triggers a service bonus, then a fresh wave starts.

Source vehicle capacity becomes covers per service wave, rather than a requirement for dozens of physical seats. The first restaurant retains 52 logical covers, including four premium covers. A small set of visible tables represents active diners. The baseline must not add a separate table-capacity or dishwashing delay: either would introduce a new bottleneck and change earnings. Full-wave completion, stage-event timestamps and admission timing must be checked against the source simulator before claiming parity.

Cash is shared across unlocked restaurants. Background restaurants retain their earnings contribution. The player chooses upgrades, opens outlets, assigns managers, starts training and decides when to renovate.

Cash and monetization baseline

Let F be the source fare coefficient renamed menu value, E standard covers and P premium covers. V = F × (10E + 60P). Allocate 30% to order deposits, 30% to kitchen fulfilment and 40% to the dining bill and tips, each multiplied by its corresponding pay upgrade and applicable modifiers. These are parts of one customer receipt, not three full-price charges. The service-wave bonus is 10% of the combined stage payout before bonus modifiers.

Keep two distinct balancing scenarios: Current as recovered fallback and New as a comparison. The first restaurant’s full nine-card progression costs approximately 844,045 Current cash or 422,016 New cash before discounts and prestige. These are completion costs, not the optimal strategy.

Cash sinks: upgrades, restaurants, research and operating licences. Gems: manager-related purchases, research slots, timer acceleration and offers. Keep source standard offline earnings at 25% of network income for at most two hours after absences longer than five minutes. The source premium idle flag uses 75% and a 24-hour cap. Calculate offline rewards from a versioned snapshot and persist claims to prevent duplicate grants.

Proposed monetization follows the source categories: rewarded income boosts and gem rewards; permanent income boosts; the enhanced offline product; gem packs; welcome offers and monthly passes. Product prices and stacking rules require live-store verification. The film contains no real purchase or advertisement. Do not add forced ads, new currency drains or extra waiting mechanics while testing economy parity.

Managers training and renovation

Retain the source content budget of 71 managers across five rarities, with original restaurant characters and the same functional role families: order manager, kitchen manager and dining manager. Managers unlock at the second restaurant, following the observed reference onboarding. Reuse the 13 repeatable research nodes capped at 100, four operating licences and eight prestige research nodes capped at 20 as a baseline; rename and validate every effect.

Completion means all 40 restaurants and their 10,800 first-pass upgrade purchases, excluding a complete manager collection, endless prestige and live events. Keep a baseline parity scenario alongside a separately named restaurant tuning scenario. Measure first upgrade, first wave, second restaurant, district licences, max-card milestones and first renovation. Compare free, rewarded-ad and purchaser cohorts separately. No new retention target or calendar completion time is asserted here.

Proposed acceptance gate: under the same spending policy and modifiers, income-event totals and milestone times should remain within 5% of the reference simulator before adding new mechanics. This is a test criterion, not a result already achieved. Profile a representative Android device before committing to a frame-rate or battery target.

Interface and animation plan

Portrait HUD: restaurant name, shared cash, estimated network income, gems and current service-wave progress. A cutaway 3D room fills the centre. Customer queues run along the entrance side, the kitchen occupies the back, and staff routes remain visible through the middle. Three upgrade tabs and three large cards sit below the restaurant. Secondary map, managers and research navigation stays outside the main food area.

Walk cycles use alternating limbs and small body bounce with speed matched to travel. Chefs chop, stir and flip food with hand anticipation and follow-through. Servers carry a stable tray while walking and lean during handoff. Guests sit, lift food, nod and give a brief satisfied reaction. Food has layered construction, plate landing and steam. Coins follow arcs toward the HUD. Upgrades get a short scale pulse and a visible station improvement.

The demo soundtrack uses original synthesized footsteps, chopping, grill sizzle, ceramic plate clinks, order confirmation, coin chimes and an upgrade flourish, with a restrained original musical bed. No reference-game audio or artwork is copied.

Production sequence and deliverables

  1. Now: this design plan, a 40-restaurant source mapping, an editable 3D concept scene and a 15-second MP4 with synchronized original sound.
  2. First playable: one real restaurant with all nine upgrades, staged customer payments, wave completion, savings, offline claims and actual touch tests. Show available upgrades clearly from the first build.
  3. Progression slice: second restaurant, background income, managers, initial research, first licence and renovation. Run the reference-policy simulator and measure any divergence.
  4. Full content: five districts, 40 restaurant layouts, recipe presentation variants, manager roster, research, prestige and balanced onboarding.
  5. Release preparation: physical Android testing, performance, privacy and consent, real billing and rewarded ads, restoration and purchase handling, release signing and store assets.

Accounts and store IDs are not needed for this concept film. Publishing credentials, ad account IDs, billing products, final application ID, release signing ownership and device testing will be needed for the production release.
