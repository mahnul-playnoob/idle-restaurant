01   GAME DESIGN DOCUMENT
Traffic Flow Game Design and Balance
Android version 0.8.1   Version code 740   4 November 2026
Traffic Flow is an idle transport management game built around boarding throughput. Players improve check in, transit and seating, collect route income, expand into new destinations and transport modes, then reset individual destinations through prestige. Its main strategic decision is where to invest the next unit of cash to improve network earnings.
This document reconstructs the packaged design and economy and provides a conditional playtime model. The strongest balance finding is the combination of exponential upgrade costs, restricted offline earnings and prestige costs that grow faster than prestige income. These systems make account power and play cadence essential to any completion estimate.

02   GAME DESIGN DOCUMENT
Player Experience and Core Loop
Fantasy and player role
The player runs an expanding transport network. Vehicles are visual production units; the primary interaction is economic management. A glowing queue passes through several services, fills a cabin or cargo layout, and generates payments. Players observe congestion and redirect spending toward the limiting stage.
Moment to moment loop
1  Passengers or cargo enter the queue and are processed at check in.
2  Units walk or move along a path and pass the boarding door.
3  Units occupy seats or loading spaces. Stage payments accrue.
4  A full vehicle completes a departure and generates its departure payment.
5  The player buys upgrades, improves throughput or pay, and repeats.
Long term loop
Cash funds destination unlocks and timed research. Certificates open additional transport modes. Managers add route and stage power. Maximized routes become eligible for prestige, producing a permanent progression currency and a higher route rank. Live events and collection systems supply parallel objectives.

Design pillars
Readable flow creates feedback. Small upgrades offer frequent decisions. New vehicle layouts change the feel of throughput. Persistent collection and prestige provide goals after the first network expansion. These are design interpretations of the recovered systems, rather than statements of developer intent.

04   GAME DESIGN DOCUMENT
Destination Structure and Progression
Main network structure
Five mode groups each define eight destinations. A destination stores a mode, body, ID, city, premium rows, economy rows, fare coefficient, cash unlock cost and map or travel parameters. The fare is a game coefficient, not a real ticket price. City IDs repeat across transport modes, so the unique key is mode plus city ID.
Capacity and payout
Premium capacity equals premium rows times premium columns. Economy capacity equals economy rows times body columns. A premium seat has six times the base pay of an economy seat in the aggregate trip payout calculation. Capacity grows unevenly between destinations; a smaller layout can still earn more because its fare coefficient is higher.

Bottleneck behavior
Increasing one service speed can move congestion to another service. A speed purchase that does not change the limiting stage can have little immediate effect on income. Pay cards increase revenue without solving congestion. The correct comparison is next cost divided by the change in total network income, rather than raw effect size.
Diminishing marginal benefits
Income multipliers rise linearly while cash costs accelerate. A pay card moving from 1.0x to 1.2x improves that component by 20%. Moving from 6.8x to 7.0x improves it by only 2.94%. Instant processing remains probabilistic at maximum level. It does not reach 100% with the raw card alone.
Evidence  kk0.a and z46.m. Raw timing outputs are interpreted as service parameters; on screen timing also depends on vehicle and account modifiers.

Before route modifiers, the level 29 purchase is about 199.9 times its level 0 price. Costs therefore become much steeper while the percentage gain from linear pay upgrades shrinks. Maxing a route can be a collection or prestige objective even when opening another route has better financial return.

12   GAME DESIGN DOCUMENT
Cash Economy and Offline Balance
Trip income construction
Let F be the fare coefficient, E the number of economy seats and P the premium seats. Base trip value V = F x (10E + 60P). Gate pay is 30% of V times its pay card. Door pay is 30% times its card. Seat pay is 40% times its card. Each component also receives the applicable stage, route, manager and account multipliers.
Departure pay is 10% of the combined stage payout before departure modifiers. Route income per minute equals total trip payout divided by predicted trip duration in minutes. Network income sums eligible unlocked route incomes, then applies global income multipliers. Inactive unlocked routes therefore matter to network economics.

Recovered offline rule
For a qualifying absence longer than 300 seconds, standard offline cash = 0.25 x network income per minute x min(absence seconds, 7200) / 60. The premium idle flag changes the multiplier to 0.75 and the cap to 86,400 seconds. Collection x2 is separately registered as a rewarded action.
At 1,000 cash per minute, a two hour standard claim yields 30,000 cash. An eight hour standard absence still yields 30,000. With the premium flag, eight hours yields 360,000, before a collection bonus. The cap difference is as important as the advertised income multiplier.
Evidence  e73.a0, dh3.w, uh3.b, o3.t, vx2 rewarded action registration and d18 wallet behavior.

Price escalation
For a repeatable node at level L, q = 1.28^0.3. Price = max(1, floor[adjusted base x q^L x (q - 1) / 0.28 x 10000^S]). S = (3 - 2x)x^2, where x is clamp((L - 20) / 60, 0, 1). Research speed has a 2.4x base adjustment and Premium crew has 1.75x. The late curve adds up to a 10,000x multiplier beyond ordinary exponential growth.
Timers and rushing
Noncertificate timers use a group curve of (1 + bL + cL^2) times the group base minutes, then divide by 1 + 0.01 times Research speed level. Timer rounding is 30 second increments below an hour and 5 minute increments at or above an hour, with a 30 second minimum. Certificate timers use their own base minutes.
Research rush gems = max(2, ceil[1.5 x remaining minutes^0.75]). More slots increase parallelism, but do not remove income gates or cash costs. Exact group constants are retained in ay5, while the workbook preserves the raw node parameters.

Recovered eligibility and reset
A destination must be the active route, have every card at its cap, and be in a safe boarding state before prestige can confirm. Confirmation increases that destination rank by one, adds one prestige coin, resets its nine card levels to zero and clears its current boarding state. The visible confirmation path does not zero the shared cash wallet or reset other routes.

Prestige research
Eight nodes are present: five mode income nodes and three stage income nodes. Each is capped at 20 levels and costs one prestige coin per purchase. Mode nodes add 5% per level, reaching 2x at level 20. Stage nodes add 10% per level, reaching 3x at level 20. Maxing all eight requires 160 coins and therefore 160 successful prestige actions, before any alternative coin grants.
Balance implication
Prestige is not merely a reset that makes every later cycle faster. Its reward is permanent account progression plus a much larger nominal route income, with steeper reinvestment costs. Evaluate the coin benefit and the effect on total network income before recommending a reset.

18   GAME DESIGN DOCUMENT
Playtime Model and Completion Definitions
What counts as completion
The game has several different completion targets: unlock every mode, unlock all 40 destinations, max every main card once, earn a chosen prestige rank, complete research, collect managers, or finish a particular event. No finite universal total playtime follows from these systems. A first network clear is finite; prestige and repeated live content extend play beyond it.
Reference run assumptions
The reference model starts with zero cash, no upgrades and Dublin unlocked. It maximizes one destination before unlocking the next in packaged order. It buys the next local upgrade with the best immediate cost divided by incremental income. Already unlocked routes continue contributing to network income. It buys certificates when needed, enforces their cash and income gates, and waits their base timers while income continues accruing.
The model omits managers, prestige, general research, free grants, ads, events, purchases and manual interaction time. A constant network income multiplier is varied only for sensitivity. It is not an assertion about purchasable stacking rules. All 10,800 local card purchases are represented.
Throughput approximation
For every route the model estimates a cycle using the maximum of check in work, door work and stowing work, plus walking and a small changeover allowance, then applies body modifiers. Raw card effects and fare based payouts are recovered. The unified throughput approximation is not the exact airplane seat simulation or a verified reproduction of all nonplane overlap rules.
Interpretation limits
These are deterministic reference estimates, not measured player hours, a speedrun bound or a forecast of a typical account. Sequentially maxing every route is one chosen policy. Expanding earlier, selecting different research, receiving rewards or prestiging can materially change the result. Throughput error also changes every affordability wait.

Illustrative calendar conversion
Three evenly spaced 10 minute sessions per day provide 30 active minutes. Three standard capped offline claims credit 360 minutes at 25%, equivalent to another 90 active minutes at unchanged income. That is roughly two equivalent online hours per day. Dividing the 423 hour reference by two gives about 211 days.
The 211 day result is only a cadence conversion. It does not simulate purchases being delayed until the next visit, changing income during absence, reward boosts or account power. It is neither a target nor a claim about observed retention. The workbook lets the reader change session frequency, duration, cap and offline efficiency.
Required measurement
A defensible player playtime distribution needs clean account cohorts by balance variant, time to each route and certificate, active minutes, offline credited minutes, spend, ad usage, research and manager power. Report median and upper percentiles for each defined completion target.

Suggested tuning experiments
These are proposed tests, not recovered game rules: aim for a visible first service improvement in the opening minute; keep several useful early purchases within the first five minutes; explain the offline cap before the first return; and present prestige with a clear before and after earnings comparison. Set later targets using real cohorts instead of assuming one global time budget.
Marginal return rule
For a candidate purchase, payback minutes = cash cost divided by incremental total income per minute. If the increment is zero, mark it as a prerequisite or collection purchase. Compare card, route and research returns on the same network basis. For prestige, include lost route income during the rebuild and the value of the coin rather than counting only the final rank multiplier.
